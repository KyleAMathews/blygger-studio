Packaging status updated 2026-11-22 for the formatting draft PR. Sources include the pinned public implementations and focused probes in [editor-behavior-audit.md](editor-behavior-audit.md), the editor/link source, and the [browser execution record](editor-oracle-design/browser-execution.md). Inline code is parked because this editor primarily serves writing. The [authoring assessment](editor-oracle-design/authoring-assessment.md) records the design before this expansion; the later reviews and reports below supply its subsequent evidence.

The 2026-11-22 [native-history scope decision](editor-oracle-design/native-history-scope-20261122.md) supersedes earlier native typing/newline Undo and compatibility-command recovery claims in the historical receipts below. The Chromium workaround and controlled provider are removed. Current laws retain immediate receiving, protected regions and Save, plus Undo/Redo of app-applied formatting. The mixed model resets its known history after browser-default typing/newline. This deliberately narrows coverage and does not claim a browser repair. Smart-list and other feature proposals remain separate.

## Feature candidates

The research shows shared conventions, not a single universal Markdown editing specification. The choices already adopted for whitespace, multiline emphasis, off-list Tab, formulas and marker-only text stay in force. Kyle Mathews (GitHub KyleAMathews) approved following main on 2026-11-21: native Enter/Shift+Enter insert ordinary newlines; the hard-break toolbar retains the adopted breakEdit command law. The [native-newline law delta](editor-oracle-design/native-newline-law-delta.md) preserves historical evidence and lists the new receiving gate. **F2–F5 remain proposed behavior changes.** More complete oracle coverage does not implement or adopt those features.

[Concrete feature-law proposals](editor-feature-law-proposals.md) now spell out F2–F5 and bounded U2 recovery, including examples, compatibility costs and adoption choices. Their [independent review](editor-oracle-design/feature-proposals-review.md) resolved ambiguous guards, mixed-line eligibility and host receipt timing. Every proposed law remains **unadopted**; review acceptance establishes a document ready for a product decision.

| Suggested order | Feature | Why consider it | Rules to settle before implementation |
|---|---|---|---|

Keep these as one coherent feature family. |

## Recommended sequence

1. Validate the included CI candidate with a complete cold Linux draft-PR run and retain its input, aggregate and cleanup receipts. Keep the stopped default campaigns and the completed focal sensitivities distinct; a baseline or selected kill is not a full mutation-portfolio verdict. Obtain the remaining exact-source mutation and loss evidence before retiring further legacy or clipboard tests. Compose loading/Retry work resumes separately after this formatting PR is packaged.
2. Resolve any remaining receiver evidence, then present U2 native-host recovery as a concrete law decision using the completed probes. Partial-mutation behavior still needs exploration. Use actual browser input/settlement boundaries, keep missing host premises distinct from product-law violations, and preserve independent semantics and fixed/random budgets.
3. Adopt concrete F2 list continuation/exit/Backspace laws as one family. Resolve numbering, selected-range splits and nested exit behavior before implementing generated structural and real-keyboard histories together.
4. Adopt F3 keyboard-placeholder commitment/escaping policy and F4 heading/quote/new-list behavior, followed by F5 quote continuation. Implement the approved behavior changes oracle-first. Inline code remains parked.
5. Expand T5 syntax-aware behavior only after its rules are explicit. Do not silently replace the current literal source-edit laws with another editor's parser choices.

The three inline campaign survivors/framework questions and the separate Workerd malformed-surrogate preview finding remain tracked in their execution records until their own evidence closes them. They are not feature proposals, and passing these bounded campaigns does not establish zero gaps across the editor.
